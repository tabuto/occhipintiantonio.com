Critical article by [Luigi Tallarico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Tallarico), published in _Secolo d'Italia_, Thursday 31 December 1987, p. 7.

> In truth, when light exalts form from within, its content is altered by that exuberance of spiritual and lyrical restlessness, as we have also noted in a fine Sicilian watercolourist. We refer to Antonio Occhipinti, born in and working from Gela, and to his Roman exhibition at Agostinelli Arte. The catalogue presentation quotes Annigoni's flattering judgement («I feel I can say with complete confidence that Occhipinti is one of the finest watercolourists in Italy»), while also identifying the artist's two preeminent qualities: luminous transparency and solidity of composition; poetic invention and adherence to reality, such that «in these works one reads History without making History».
>
> From these brief critical remarks emerges the unified vision of an artist who, within the specificity of the medium, has managed to introduce a distinctive element to be found in the absorbed and at once lucid penetration of light, so that the image gains spiritual depth and does not dissipate in the watery vibration of surface glazes.
>
> On the other hand, the noted «formal solidity» — which does not neglect the historicity of its content (a Sicilian identity renewed in the present, without the heavy, plaintive weight of local memory) — does not marshal lyrical transparencies to mimic natural atmospheres, but rather to render plausible the conflicts of the depths. Indeed, Occhipinti, while in his graphic analysis of decorative descent he recovers Annigoni's classic motifs (more as mastery of line than as recourse to ideological and/or stylistic meaning), in the absorbed penetration of light reveals instead the destiny of a substance that is not exhausted in the executive act of a more or less precious layering of watery strata.
>
> Indeed the artist links to the essential component of watercolour a research that is at once constructive and subterranean, visionary and introspective, more signifying than signified, so as to bind new linguistic and expressive solutions to this specific traditional medium. It is precisely through the autonomous and inventive function of form-light that Occhipinti manages to overturn the cornerstones of a technique usually exercised in the layered representation of a reality that coincides with form — in order to recover instead the deepest, and intact, lyrical values of the reality within.

_**Luigi Tallarico**, Secolo d'Italia, 31 December 1987_
